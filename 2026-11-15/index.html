Week of Nov 8 – Nov 15, 2026

Autonomy Weekly: AV, automotive AI & SDV

Tesla made its pivot explicit: Cybercab went on sale to individuals while the Cybertruck got an end date, and Robotaxi grew to eight cities running 24/7. Waymo, still the leader, drew a new federal recall query over school-bus passing.

Top headlines

1

Tesla opens Cybercab pre-orders for personal use

Select customers can order the Cybercab at $29,990 plus a $299/month fee; delivery estimated December 2026 in a few Texas cities, geofenced operation only. Musk replied the fee is too high and should be about $115/month. TechCrunch, Nov 14

Why it matters: First steering-wheel-free car sold to consumers: Tesla is testing whether owners will pay for personal unsupervised autonomy.

2

Cybertruck gets an end date, to make room for Cybercab

Musk said on Nov 6 that Giga Texas would wind down Cybertruck production around mid-2027, then on Nov 11 extended it through the end of summer 2027 to work through the backlog. Electrek reports prices rose up to 10% and base Dual Motor orders were paused (cheapest now $92,490). The Verge, Nov 6 Electrek, Nov 11

Why it matters: For a Cybertruck owner: resale and long-term support questions; no statement on support for existing trucks appears in these sources.

3

Robotaxi: San Antonio launch, bigger Austin, 24/7 everywhere

Tesla launched in San Antonio on Nov 9 (its 8th city, about 273 sq mi vs Waymo's 59). Austin's area grew to about 443 sq mi, and on Nov 11 Tesla said Robotaxi runs 24/7 in every city it serves. Mashable, Nov 10 Axios San Antonio, Nov 9 Not a Tesla App TeslaNorth, Nov 11

Why it matters: Coverage is now huge on paper, but Axios noted a tracker showed just one Tesla robotaxi in San Antonio at launch.

4

Texas register: Tesla briefly passed Waymo

Tesla Robotaxi led Waymo 1,302 to 1,297 registered Texas AVs on Nov 10; by early Nov 15 Waymo was back ahead, 1,321 to 1,306. EVwire, Nov 11 EVwire Tracker, Nov 15

Why it matters: The best public fleet scorecard shows the two now neck and neck in Texas. Registered does not mean in service.

5

FSD v14.3.13 keeps rolling; Finland approves it as Tesla Assisted Driving

Build 2026.38.5.13 (Nov 13) carries FSD v14.3.13 with a rewritten MLIR compiler cited for 20% faster reaction time. Finland recognised the Dutch approval on Nov 10; the speed offset is capped at 10% and an EU-wide vote is expected this winter. Teslascope Not a Tesla App updates Traficom, Nov 11

Why it matters: This is the software in your truck, and Europe is the next big subscription market.

6

NHTSA opens a recall query into about 3,000 Waymo vehicles

Regulators want to know why Waymo robotaxis kept passing stopped school buses after a software fix completed in Nov 2025. Separately, ABC7 reported video of a Waymo trying to get around a CHP traffic break on I-280. Reuters via Investing.com, Nov 9 TechCrunch, Nov 9 ABC7, Nov 12

Why it matters: The leader's safety record is under fresh review, and it sets the bar for how regulators judge any AV software fix, Tesla's included.

7

Zoox starts rides in Atlanta and grows in Las Vegas

Employee, family and friends rides began in northwest Atlanta on Nov 13; Las Vegas service expanded to Downtown and the Arts District with 6 a.m.–3 a.m. hours. Zoox, Nov 13 Zoox, Nov 11

Why it matters: The other purpose-built robotaxi (Amazon) is widening its footprint.

8

Compute: NVIDIA Alpamayo 2 Edge, Kakao L4 deal, Wayve on CoreWeave

NVIDIA's 2B-parameter reasoning driving model runs on DRIVE AGX Thor at 160–190 ms pixel-to-trajectory. Kakao Mobility signed an L4 MOU using dual Thor; Wayve signed a multi-year Blackwell training deal with CoreWeave. NVIDIA Technical Blog, Nov 10 Automotive World, Nov 9 Automotive World, Nov 9

Why it matters: NVIDIA is becoming the default car computer and training cloud for the non-Tesla field.

Analysis

Our interpretation, based on the sourced items above.

  • Tesla is moving from selling cars to selling autonomy: Cybercab pre-orders plus the Cybertruck end date make that explicit.
  • Its robotaxi footprint is growing fastest on paper, but service areas are outrunning vehicles in new cities; Waymo still leads on cities and fleet.
  • Waymo's school-bus query shows regulators will judge AV software fixes by real-world results, which applies to every driverless operator, Tesla included.
  • Outside Tesla, NVIDIA is consolidating the stack: in-car compute (Thor), models (Alpamayo) and training cloud partners.
