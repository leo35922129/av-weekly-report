Week of Nov 9 – Nov 16, 2026

Autonomy Weekly: AV, automotive AI & SDV

NVIDIA founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov 12 after reports of an affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, leaving the leading AV compute supplier under an interim CEO going into Tuesday's earnings. For Tesla, the Texas fleet race is still neck and neck (Waymo 1,321 registered vs Tesla 1,306), FSD v14.3.13 is reaching owners' trucks with a new collision-evasion feature, and Cybercab rides in Dallas have not started yet.

Top headlines

1

FSD v14.3.13 spreads to owners; Automatic Collision Evasion first seen on a Cybertruck

BASENOR, citing Teslascope, reports build 2026.38.5.13 is rolling out in waves (about 5,720 more vehicles in five minutes on Nov 14, about 17,380 more on Nov 15) with FSD v14.3.13 for 100% of eligible AI4 vehicles in each wave. It also lists Automatic Collision Evasion, a steer-to-avoid safety feature said to work even when FSD is off, first spotted on a Cybertruck AWD in Florida. BASENOR, Nov 13 (updated Nov 15)

Why it matters: This is the software for your truck. The feature details come from a secondary outlet quoting Teslascope posts, not from Tesla release notes we could open, so treat specifics as reported.

2

Texas register: Waymo 1,321, Tesla 1,306, and Cybercab Dallas still pending

The Texas DMV register, as of 12:14 a.m. CT on Nov 16, shows Waymo 1,321 and Tesla Robotaxi 1,306 out of 3,452 registered AVs from 17 companies (Avride 344, Aurora 91, Zoox 63). Not a Tesla App says Cybercab rides are still limited to Austin, with Dallas planned this month. EVwire Tracker, Nov 16 Not a Tesla App, Nov 12

Why it matters: Both counts are unchanged from a day earlier, so the fleet race is flat this week. Registered does not mean in service.

3

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO

NVIDIA said on Nov 12 that Huang resigned as president, CEO and director effective immediately. AP and TechCrunch tie it to his admitted affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive suspected of spying for China. Kress stays CFO while serving as interim CEO, Harvey Jones becomes the first board chair, and a CEO search is under way. Q3 results are still scheduled for Nov 17. NVIDIA press release, Nov 12 TechCrunch, Nov 12 AP, Nov 12 StockTitan NVDA 8-K filings

Why it matters: NVIDIA supplies DRIVE Thor and the Alpamayo models used across the AV industry; leadership uncertainty is the week's biggest industry shock, though the sources show no automotive product change yet.

4

Baidu Q3: Apollo Go at 30 cities and 390M+ km, but ads drag earnings

Baidu reported on Nov 16 that Apollo Go reached 30 cities as of October, logged about 34 million autonomous km in Q3 and more than 390 million cumulative, with Shenzhen fully driverless rides up about 170% quarter on quarter and the Dubai fleet doubled. Online marketing fell 22% and adjusted EPS of 6.78 yuan missed the 7.67 consensus. Baidu Q3 2026 release, Nov 16 Investing.com, Nov 16

Why it matters: Apollo Go keeps scaling abroad while the parent's ad business shrinks; Baidu did not disclose a total ride count in this release.

5

XPeng Q3: record revenue, 169,000 to 174,000 Q4 deliveries guided, robotaxi cleared for Guangzhou

XPeng reported Nov 16 record revenue of 24.28 billion yuan (up 19.1%), 118,390 deliveries and a narrower 870 million yuan net loss. It guided Q4 deliveries to 169,000 to 174,000. Its Oct 26 approval covers L4 Robotaxi demonstration operation across Guangzhou, initially by invitation code, using four in-house Turing chips (3,000 TOPS) and VLA 2.0. CnEVPost, Nov 16 XPeng press release, Oct 26

Why it matters: XPeng says it will be a technology provider to robotaxi partners rather than run big fleets, a different model from Tesla and Waymo.

6

Pony.ai and Toyota put the Gen-7 bZ4X robotaxi into driverless service

Pony.ai said on Nov 16 that the bZ4X Robotaxi joined its fully driverless commercial fleet in Guangzhou, bookable 24 hours a day, with about 1,000 bZ4X robotaxis planned across major Chinese cities. Pony.ai reports Q3 results on Nov 18. Pony.ai blog, Nov 16 Pony.ai IR, Oct 20

Why it matters: Mass-produced, OEM-built L4 vehicles are how Chinese operators cut cost per car, the lever Tesla is chasing with Cybercab.

7

Waymo: DC bill stalls, Raleigh-Durham talks, school-bus query still open

A D.C. Council committee tied 3-3 on Nov 12, so the commercial AV bill did not advance; Waymo keeps mapping Northern Virginia. A Waymo policy adviser told a North Carolina summit it is in talks about Raleigh and Durham, per CARC. NHTSA's school-bus recall query from Nov 9 remains open, and Waymo says it has had no citations since March. WJLA, Nov 12 CARC, Nov 13 Reuters via StreetInsider, Nov 9 TechCrunch, Nov 9

Why it matters: Expansion now depends as much on city and state politics as on the driver.

8

Europe's Tesla Assisted Driving path: 11 countries, EU vote no earlier than December

Finland became the 11th country to recognise the Dutch RDW approval. Those 11 account for only about 15% of the EU population, a qualified majority needs 15 states and 65%, and Germany plus Italy would still reach only about 47%. Separately, NHTSA extended the comment deadline on its FMVSS 108 proposal for vehicles without manual controls to Dec 10. Automotive World, Nov 11 Federal Register, Nov 16

Why it matters: Europe is the next big FSD market, but the EU-wide vote is the real gate, and the European product is being trimmed to get there.

Tesla FSD & Robotaxi

  • FSD v14.3.13. Build 2026.38.5.13 was first spotted on a Cybertruck AWD in Florida on Nov 13 and, per Teslascope counts quoted by BASENOR, reached waves of thousands of cars on Nov 14 and 15. It carries FSD v14.3.13 for eligible AI4 cars and a security update. BASENOR also lists Automatic Collision Evasion, which steers to avoid or soften an imminent collision independent of FSD; it says the feature appeared in earlier 2026.38.5.x notes. BASENOR, Nov 13 (updated Nov 15)
  • FSD v15. Not a Tesla App says Tesla planned 24/7 Robotaxi alongside a fleet-wide v15 rollout in October, that the timeline slipped by weeks, and that Tesla has not said whether v15 now runs on the whole Robotaxi fleet. Not a Tesla App, Nov 12
  • Texas fleet scoreboard. As of 12:14 a.m. CT on Nov 16, Waymo had 1,321 registered AVs and Tesla Robotaxi 1,306; Avride 344 and Aurora 91 follow. The register covers 3,452 vehicles from 17 companies. EVwire Tracker, Nov 16
  • 24/7 detail. Hours grew from 6am-10pm in August to round the clock this week, and Musk's grey-kitten-on-grey-tarmac night challenge explains the gradual path. Cybercab rides are still Austin-only, with Dallas planned this month. Not a Tesla App, Nov 12
  • Dallas Cybercab timing. Tesla said on Oct 21 that unsupervised Cybercab rides would open to the public in Dallas in November, with no exact date; Austin Cybercab rides began Sept 3. Where's the Driver, Nov 1
  • Cybercab compliance. NHTSA opened audit query AQ26002 on Sept 3 into Tesla's self-certification of the control-free Cybercab; Tesla's response was due Oct 30 and the source found no public outcome. Where's the Driver, Nov 1
  • Owner Cybercab. Tesla's Ashok Elluswamy said a personally owned Cybercab will work unsupervised in all the same geographies as Robotaxi, serving the owner's needs; Musk said the $299 monthly fee should be about $115. GetTesla, Nov 14 Reuters via Indian Express, Nov 15
  • Musk and Cybertruck. Benzinga's weekly roundup says Musk predicted Cybercab will dominate ride-hailing where Robotaxi can operate commercially, and that tuner Unplugged Performance offered to take over the remaining Cybertruck line and build a three-row SUV from it. Benzinga, Nov 15
  • Existing safety probe. NHTSA's engineering analysis EA26002 (upgraded Jul 2) covers FSD's ability to detect and alert drivers to degraded visibility such as glare, fog and dust, and cites nine crashes. It is an open investigation, not a recall. NHTSA letter EA26002, Jul 2

Analysis

Our interpretation, based on the sourced items above.

  • NVIDIA's leadership shock is the week's biggest industry story; the sources show no change to automotive products yet, so Tuesday's call under Kress is the first test of continuity.
  • Tesla's fleet gap with Waymo in Texas is only 15 registered vehicles, and 24/7 operation plus the Dallas Cybercab launch are the next things that could move it.
  • Chinese operators are scaling through partners and mass-produced vehicles (Pony.ai with Toyota, XPeng as a technology provider, Baidu abroad), while Tesla and Waymo scale their own fleets.
  • Europe's hurdle for Tesla is political arithmetic, not just software: small-country approvals do not add up to the 65% population threshold.
